# Pack design

TerraFirmaCraft on a real-scale Earth, mechanized with Create, industrialized with GregTech,
with Create Aeronautics for travel. Minecraft 1.21.1, NeoForge.

Decided 2026-10-06. The progression checker (`tools/recipes/progression.json`) encodes these
decisions as rules; when a rule breaks, the explorer shows the shortcut.

## Decisions

| Area | Decision |
|---|---|
| Era order | **TFC → Create → GregTech.** TFC carries stone → copper → bronze → wrought iron → steel; Create mechanizes from the bronze age; GregTech's steam age builds on Create. Iron and steel stay TFC milestones whichever mod's machines do the work: wrought iron comes only from the bloomery (anything iron that's smelted or melted becomes cast iron, as in TFC), and steel only from TFC pig iron (GregTech's primitive blast furnace refines pig iron instead of iron). |
| Unification | **TFC items win** for every metal TFC has (copper, bronze family, brass, tin, zinc, nickel, silver, gold, wrought/pig/cast iron, the steels), over vanilla too. Exceptions: vanilla lapis and emerald win, because enchanting accepts only the vanilla lapis and other mods' recipes the vanilla emerald (villagers no longer trade emeralds: see *Villagers*). **Iron means TFC wrought iron**: Almost Unified maps iron ingots, nuggets, blocks, plates and rods onto wrought iron. Ore blocks aren't unified; TFC's per-rock ores are the real ores. GregTech items cover materials TFC doesn't have. TFC's bituminous coal counts as coal (`#c:gems/coal`, lignite doesn't), so GregTech's coke oven and other coal recipes run on it, and Almost Unified turns vanilla coal outputs into TFC coal. |
| GregTech ores | **Off.** GregTech's ore veins are switched off everywhere (TFC's overworld never placed them; the Nether and End would have). GregTech's raw materials come from TFC ores: the macerator turns TFC ore pieces into GregTech crushed ore at twice TFC's yield (the way it doubles a vanilla raw ore; one crushed ore per 50 mB of TFC metal), and mineral and gem ores count as a GregTech ore block (1 → 2 crushed). From there GregTech's usual chain applies. GregTech-only minerals come from its processing chains, from the sea floor (see *Deep sea*: the one exception the author approved) or later from planets, never from new land veins (see *Overworld*). GregTech-only elements come out of TFC ores that carry them in real deposits, as GregTech by-products (`kubejs/startup_scripts/ore_byproducts.js`), so they appear with LV machines (ore washer, thermal centrifuge, centrifuge) and GregTech's own chains do the rest: tetrahedrite gives realgar (arsenic), magnetite gives vanadium magnetite (vanadium) and ilmenite (rutile, then titanium at HV), cassiterite gives monazite (rare earths: neodymium, samarium, cerium, yttrium, lanthanum) and scheelite (tungsten at EV), malachite gives molybdenite (molybdenum). They replace by-products those ores' own metals or other TFC ores already supply (iron, tin, zinc, bismuth, gold, calcite). With these the checker reaches the IV hull. |
| Difficulty | **Moderate.** TFC survival as designed, GregTech at default settings, Create gated but not punishing. |
| Create opens | **Bronze age.** Andesite alloy is made from TFC igneous extrusive rock or diorite (andesite, basalt, dacite, rhyolite or diorite, raw or cobble; extrusive rock can be kilometres from a start, diorite dikes are within a few hundred blocks of almost any, see *Known gaps*) + zinc or bronze nuggets, so water wheels, shafts and millstones arrive early. Create uses TFC sheets wherever it used metal plates, and the mechanical press takes a wrought iron double sheet, so heavy machines (press, mixer, deployer, saw) need TFC wrought iron sheets. Create's belts, spout, funnels, tunnels and pulleys take leather or TFC burlap cloth (or either kind of dried kelp) instead of vanilla dried kelp, which TFC worlds lack. |
| GregTech steam | **Fire bricks + a Create mechanical pump.** Every GregTech boiler (coal, lava, solar; low and high pressure) needs a Create mechanical pump, and the vanilla furnace in GregTech's recipes becomes TFC fire bricks, so GregTech's steam age can't skip Create. With the pump being a bronze-age part, steam starts in the late bronze age (checker: first boiler at step 15, after the pump at 14 and before wrought iron at 19). GregTech's high-pressure steam and LV electrics need TFC steel. |
| Airships | **With Create's brass tier.** Every way to get airborne needs brass (TFC brass = copper + zinc): lift (the hot-air burner and steam vent take brass plates, envelopes brass nuggets, about one ingot per 18 envelopes) and thrust (every propeller sits on a brass casing). Simulated's portable engine takes a brass plate and TFC fire bricks. Ground vehicles and the physics assembler stay brass-free. The burner and propeller bearing also need wrought iron plates, so the first airship flies just after the bloomery (checker: burner and propeller bearing step 21, wrought iron 19). Levitite needs End stone and stays out of reach. |
| Create ↔ GregTech | **Through recipes**, no extra mods. Create makes GregTech's early intermediates: the press makes GregTech plates from ingots (one for one, like GregTech's bender), the saw slits plates into wires (two per plate, like the wiremill), and crushing wheels and the millstone crush TFC ores into GregTech crushed ore (crushing wheels at the macerator's rate, the millstone at TFC's yield). GregTech's LV components are built from Create parts, like for like: motors around Create shafts, pumps around a Create mechanical pump, pistons geared with a Create cogwheel. And all GregTech power comes from boilers (steam turbines for electricity), so nothing in GregTech runs without Create. The electric blast furnace is built from TFC's blast furnace instead of the vanilla one. |
| World scale | **20 m per block** (map radius 862,198 × 419,647 blocks, about 1.7 million blocks east to west; Rome to Naples is 9,500 blocks). TFC: Real World caps its configured radius at 100,000 (~172 m per block) but reads each world's own `serverconfig/tfc_real_world/server.toml` without a cap, so `kubejs/startup_scripts/world_scale.js` writes that file for a new world just before the mod would copy `config/tfc_real_world/common.toml` into it, with 20 m in place of the default (and create-world maximum) 100,000 × 50,000. Existing worlds and any other scale picked on the create-world tab are left alone. Costs: about 180 MB more RAM for the mod's whole-map caches and about 16 s more world loading. True 1:1 isn't possible: those caches would need ~35 billion cells (Java arrays stop at 2.1 billion), and the source maps are 3.5 km (coastlines) to 28 km (climate, tectonics) per pixel at that scale. Ore and rock density per area are unchanged, and so are TFC's rock patches (about 1 km across, set in blocks); climate zones, the volcanic and mountain rock zones (from the tectonics map) and travel distances grow. |
| Overworld | **Frozen once the world is declared complete, so mods can then be added without world wipes.** Until then generation is still being built (caves, structures, settlements, oceans) and may change freely; `tools/worldgen_check.py` keeps recording it in the baseline, so "complete" is exactly the baseline at that point, and the rules below apply from then on. Players start anywhere on a huge map, so chunks keep generating for a world's whole life, next to terrain players already explored. The overworld is TFC with TFC: Real World's Earth and the pack's own caves (see *Caves*), plus GregTech's rubber trees in swamps (GregTech's early rubber comes from their resin); plus the pack's own ruins (see *Lived-in*), the structure mods' villages, dungeons and mineshafts (see *Structure mods*) and ArborFirmaCraft's forests (they replace most of TFC's trees: accepted by the author before the freeze); nothing else generates there, and Create's and GregTech's other worldgen targets `#minecraft:is_overworld`, which holds no TFC biome (TFC Structure Compat's additions to it and to thirteen other `c:is_*` tags are undone with data, so worldgen keyed on those tags stays as it was). Forests in new chunks depend on the order neighbouring chunks finish generating in, so they are never identical between two runs of the same pack (measured: the same four golden areas twice gave different trees in 80 of 648 chunks, and one chunk at a time in a fixed order twice 21 and 22 of 81 in the dense karst and mountain areas against 78 and 80 of 81 when the order was reversed); adding a feature to the vegetal decoration step of every biome reshuffles forests in new chunks as well (measured: 79 and 81 of 81 chunks changed), while the same addition to one biome (the deep sea's arc vents) or to `underground_structures` in every biome (the campsite) did not, and old chunks are untouched either way. `worldgen_check.py --golden` therefore compares trees as totals per area (logs, leaves, twigs, and the dominant species) with a tolerance well above that noise, and not block by block. Changes that break continuity with explored terrain (TFC or TFC: Real World code, map or scale, rock layers, carvers, ore veins, noise, sea level) are refused, so TFC and TFC: Real World are only updated when `tools/worldgen_check.py` shows no terrain change. Additions that only affect newly generated chunks (a plant, a small structure, fauna) are accepted case by case, recorded with a reason in `tools/worldgen_baseline.json`, or stripped with a data override. New material goes into new dimensions instead: GregTech-only ores will come from planets, not land veins (the sea floor's deposits, *Deep sea*, were approved as a new-chunks-only addition). Removing a mod deletes its blocks and items from existing worlds, so mods are added freely but only removed with a migration. |
| Caves | **Fewer, bigger and more varied, with decoration**, all as data (`kubejs/data/pack/`, overrides in `kubejs/data/tfc/`; notes in `docs/caves.md`). TFC's spaghetti and noodle tunnels are off and its cheese chambers are twice the size and fewer. A noise on a ~2 km scale gives each region one of three styles: chambers, great caverns (a couple of hundred blocks across, around y −20) or shafts and pits. TFC's carvers are fewer and bigger (rooms, deep caves, and canyons deeper and wider, into chasms), and a karst carver adds big caves that only cut limestone, dolomite, chalk and marble. Decoration: TFC mossy cobble with moss carpets and glow-berry cave vines in warm, wet caves (glow berries are a TFC fruit that decays), glow lichen below y 0, calcite stalactites under karst ceilings, TFC's own ice caves (which TFC ships but doesn't use) where it is −4 °C or colder, and denser rock spikes and columns. Measured on 1 km squares at Rome, Tokyo, Yakutsk and a Lhasa mountain start (same seed, before → after): cave volume 14.9–19.0 → 8.9–11.8 blocks per column (−33 to −51%); separate cave systems 468–511 → 124–305 per 100 chunks; a straight 1×2 mining tunnel hits a cave within 32 blocks 21–30% → 11–16% of the time; volume per block of cave wall (a roominess measure) 1.7–1.9 → 2.1–2.3; ore showing on cave walls about halves; generation speed unchanged within noise (Rome: 66.6 vs 69.7 chunks/s, runs spread ±5). Cave shape is continuity-breaking, so it lands once, before the first long-lived world, recorded as terrain in `tools/worldgen_baseline.json`. Lush caves use TFC's per-rock mossy cobble rather than vanilla moss blocks so that `worldgen_check.py --golden`'s rock census stays stable between runs. No cave mod: those that reshape caves or add cave biomes go through vanilla's noise router, biome source or TerraBlender, which TFC's own generator and biome source bypass (YUNG's Cave Biomes, Alex's Caves, Tectonic, Terralith, Biomes O' Plenty), YUNG's Better Caves assumes vanilla's aquifer (crash risk with TFC's), and WF's Cave Overhaul only targets `#minecraft:is_overworld` and is unmaintained. Basalt lava tubes (a basalt-only carver) were tried and dropped: too few and too small to notice. |
| Oceans | **Deeper, inside the world's existing height** (bottom y -64, sea level 63, dimension type and noise settings untouched; `pack_world` 1.4.0). TFC hard-codes each ocean biome's floor as a pair of offsets from sea level given to `BiomeNoise.ocean` and `oceanTrench` (the floor noise runs between 63 + min and 63 + max), and TFC: Real World only chooses which biome goes where from its tectonics map, so the pack moves the pairs: ocean (-26, -12) to (-32, -14) (floor y 37-51 to 31-49), deep ocean (-46, -30) to (-95, -65) (y 17-33 to -32 to -2) and the trench (-60, -46) to (-112, -95) (y 3-17 to -49 to -32, and TFC's ridged term takes it up to 16 lower); the reef (-16, -8) is as it was. A pair is remapped as a whole, so every biome that shares one moves with it: the atolls and the volcanic arc with the ocean, the deep atolls and the ridge's floor with the deep ocean. **Where:** `mod/src/main/java/pack/world/mixin/OceanDepthMixin.java` wraps the two noise builders and `oceanRidge`, with the table in `pack/world/ocean/OceanDepths.java`; `AtollFootMixin` and `StratovolcanoFootMixin` deal with what the move uncovers. **What else had to move:** the ridge lays a profile in absolute y (13 to 51) over the deep floor, which on a floor 42 blocks lower would be a plateau at y 13 under the whole biome, so it is rebuilt as the new deep floor plus the old ridge's height over the old floor (crest, flanks and axis keep their relief: crest y 49 to 12). An atoll's profile starts at y 3 and a stratovolcano's skirt ends in a flat foot at y -1 (the arc) or 11 (the volcanic coasts and islands), laid over the floor as `max(floor, profile)` and always under the old floors; on the new ones they were ledges of up to 55 blocks round every atoll and every volcano cell that reaches a trench (the steepest 4-block terrain step in the trench window was 13.5 blocks per block before the fix, 4.0 after). The foot is now never more than 22 blocks under the floor it stands on and the top is unchanged; where the floor is high (the arc's own y 31-49) nothing moves. **Measured** (TFC's own height, 4-block steps, six 2,048-block windows: Lisbon's shelf, the mid-Atlantic ridge and the Mariana ridge, the Mariana trench with its arc, an arc island, Hawaii; same seed before and after; the biomes' interiors, y): ocean 40-45 (mean 42.5) to 35-42 (38.5), deep ocean 20-27 (23.4) to -24 to -11 (-17.6), trench -6 to 9 (2.1) to -57 to -40 (-47.7, lowest -63), atolls' floor 25 to -13.5 with their rims unchanged at 60-66, ridge crest 49 to 12. **Slopes** (the generated sea floor, mean profile across the boundary): shelf to deep ocean drops 19 blocks over about 100 before and 58 after (steepest mean step 0.28 to 0.69, 15 to 35 degrees; terrain steps of 45 degrees or more 0.04% of the shelf window to 0.65%, none of 63 degrees or more), arc to trench 47 to 92 blocks over about 92 (0.60 to 1.34), and where TFC: Real World puts deep water straight against a volcanic island (Hawaii) the rise is 74 blocks in about 72 (1.64 to 2.39, a few 4-block steps of 5): a sea cliff, not a wall. **Caves:** the great caverns do break through the new floors, and the breaches are water-filled as before, because TFC floods everything within 24 blocks under an ocean floor with sea water: columns with a flooded cave under the floor are 9-15% of ocean columns before and after (trench 9.8% to 1.8%), sea water lying directly on air 0.1-3.8 per 1,000 columns before and after, lava touching sea water no higher anywhere except the trench. **Trench:** its lowest terrain is under y -54, TFC's lava level, where every open cell is lava: about 10% of trench columns have a flat floor at y -54 (mud or hardened rock, with lava under it in half of them), lava touching water goes from 0.3 to 7 per 1,000 trench columns and 0.016% of the columns end on bedrock; a trench pair of about (-101, -84) would keep the lowest floor at -53. Kelp, coral and floor blocks are as they were (mud on 81-94% of the floor, the same plants per column; leafy kelp -3% on the shelf). Generation speed is unchanged (63.6 chunks/s before, 63.5 after, mean of the six windows; runs differ by about 10). A deliberate break with every ocean chunk already generated (453 of 1,980 fixed points change height, every one in an ocean biome, none on land, none in biome, rock or climate; two of the eight golden areas, the coast and the desert coast, change), recorded as terrain in `tools/worldgen_baseline.json`; checked by `smoke.py`, `startat_test.py` and `worldgen_check.py --golden`. |
| Deep sea | **The sea floor carries the ores of the GregTech-only elements that had no source, harvested by divers.** This revises *GregTech ores* ("never from new overworld veins") for the sea floor only; the author approved it. Five full-cube blocks and one loose piece (`kubejs/startup_scripts/sea_floor.js`; the cubes are not waterloggable, so none puts vanilla water into a TFC ocean; the loose `pack:nodule` is kubejs-tfc's `tfc:groundcover` block, which is TFC's own `GroundcoverBlock`, fluid-loggable with TFC salt water). Textures (16x16, TFC-like palettes) and the three non-vanilla models are generated by `tools/sea_textures.py` into `kubejs/assets/pack/`, which also renders a review sheet drop GregTech's own raw ores through plain block loot tables (`kubejs/data/pack/loot_table/blocks/`), which GregTech's macerator already turns into crushed ore, so no recipe was written and the checker found the routes itself. **Placement is data**: placed features in `kubejs/data/pack/worldgen/{configured,placed}_feature/ocean/`, added to the biomes through TFC's own per-biome tags (`kubejs/data/tfc/tags/worldgen/placed_feature/in_biome/large_features/{ocean_ridge,deep_ocean,deep_ocean_atolls,oceanic_volcanic_arc}.json`), not TFC veins (those sit in the bedrock, have no biome filter and no surface step). **The deposits:** `pack:sulfide_chimney` (a black smoker: columns 2 to 7 blocks tall on a `pack:sulfide_mound`; raw chalcopyrite 40, pyrite 25, sphalerite 20 and galena 15 by weight, one or two per block), on the mid-ocean ridge's axis; `pack:anhydrite_chimney` (a white smoker: raw gypsum 88, sphalerite 8, pollucite 4), in the volcanic arc; `pack:nodule_bed` (mud with nodules: one raw pyrolusite, 6% cobaltite), a small rich core (discs of radius 2 to 5, was 4 to 8) on the deep ocean floor and on the ridge's flanks 400 blocks and more from its axis, inside a scatter of loose `pack:nodule` pieces (170 tries within 12 blocks of the core's centre, only where the cell is TFC salt water and the block below is mud or the bed; `tfc:block_with_fluid`, the way TFC places its mollusks) that are picked up by hand and drop what a bed block does (`kubejs/data/pack/loot_table/blocks/nodule.json`); `pack:mn_crust` (one raw pyrolusite, 20% cobaltite, 4% pitchblende), on bare ridge and arc rock. Chimneys give light 0.5 (black) or 0.4 (white) and their tip face is fullbright (`neoforge_data` in `kubejs/assets/pack/models/block/*_chimney_shape.json`). Smoke, bubbles and a quiet rumble are client-side (`kubejs/client_scripts/sea_vents.js`: once a second the chunk sections within two chunks that hold a chimney are scanned for tips, and the nearest 40 emit large smoke (black) or campfire smoke (white) and bubble-column particles, the nearest one a sound): the earlier server puffs from a random tick fired once per about 68 s per block (3 random ticks per 16^3 section per tick), too rare to see, and the server now does nothing for vents. **The ridge's axis:** TFC's `tfc:ocean_ridge` placement passes where the warped edge distance, in blocks from the axis, lies in a range; the ridge biome is the whole region around the axis (more than 2,000 blocks across; 6,886 of 9,458 chunks of a 1,536-block window at the Mariana ridge), and a probe of one block type per distance band found 273 chunks at 0 to 25 blocks, 368 at 26 to 60, 722 at 61 to 130 and 1,570 at 131 to 300: about 10.4 chunks per block of distance, a straight axis about 1,330 blocks long in that window, and 9.8 chunks per km of axis in the band 0 to 24 that the vents use. **Density, measured** (a vent field is a mound, discs of radius 3 to 6, with 14 tries of chimneys within 9 blocks): rarity 300 per chunk of the band, so one field per 30.6 km of axis expected (about three times a real ridge's one per 60 to 100 km); a 2,048-block window of the ridge held 2 fields with 117 chimney blocks (y -8 to 10) whose neighbours are only salt water (394 faces), chimney, mound and rock (no air, no vanilla water). Arc vents (rarity 300 per chunk of arc biome): 16 fields in 6,619 arc chunks, one per 42 km^2 of arc. Nodule beds, before the loose nodules: 11.3% of the columns of a deep-ocean window (715,354 blocks in 1,536 x 1,536 blocks at 12 N 135 W). **Loose nodules** (`lab.py place`, the same window, 3,063 fields): bed core 248,272 blocks (4.6% of the columns), loose nodules 378,900, together 627,172 against 688,840 for the old beds in the lab (91%: 205 against 225 pieces of raw ore per field); a real generation of a 512-block window there (`pregen.py --radius 256`, 1,138 chunks) gave 28,037 bed blocks in 4.5% of the columns and 42,060 nodules, 0.24 pieces per column against the lab's 0.26 (the old beds' 0.30); all nodules hold salt water, 34,561 sit on mud and 7,268 on the bed, 231 on hardened basalt (not traced: the placement asked for mud or the bed below), no vanilla water, 36 faces touch air (the water-air edges of cave breaches the terrain already has); crust: 0.62% of a ridge window's columns (70,061 blocks). Generation speed is unchanged (74 chunks/s at an ocean window with the ruins; runs differ by about 10). **Chains** (checker steps, the newest dump against `exploration`; 13,733 reachable items before, 13,984 after): the raw ores are step 15 (copper diving helmet 11, copper backtank 14, a pickaxe or shovel: the ocean is open to copper divers, there is no depth or pressure mechanic, only air, and red steel diving gear does not make lava safe, since Create asks for all four slots to be fire-resistant), crushed ore 19. **Niobium** dust 38 (macerating refined pyrolusite gives 14% niobium dust, so it comes with LV machines; niobium nitride dust 59, hot niobium ingot 67, niobium titanium ingot 82). **Indium** dust 65 (purified sphalerite and galena with sulfuric acid in an HV mixer, then an EV chemical reactor with aluminium; galena only comes from the vents; indium gallium phosphide 66). **Caesium** dust 19 (macerating crushed pollucite, 14%; it has no use in this GregTech). **Uranium** 235 and 238 dust 60 (pitchblende to uraninite dust 37 in a thermal centrifuge, then uranium hexafluoride and an electrolyzer; the fluorine comes from biotite dust, step 37, in an MV electrolyzer, about 65 dust per bucket of hexafluoride: no fluorite source, on purpose). **What else moved:** manganese dust 45 to 19 and manganese ingot 46 to 20 (accepted: macerating raw pyrolusite gives 14% manganese dust); stainless steel dust 46 to 45, ingot 47 to 46, plate 48 to 47 and the HV machine hull 50 to 49 follow (the MV mixer holds it near 45); of the stack-upgrade ladder only Sophisticated Storage's tier 2 and Sophisticated Backpacks' tier 1 move (49 to 48; the rest stay at 29, 40, 66, 76, 80 and 81); as by-products of the new ores: thorium 26 to 19 (a dead end: ingots, plates, rods), pyrite dust 37 to 19, gypsum dust 37 to 18, yellow garnet 59 to 19, pyrolusite dust 70 to 37, tantalite dust 45 to 37, tantalum dust 46 to 45, magnetic neodymium 53 to 52. The first design had barite in the white smoker, which moved barium 71 to 45 and the cuprate superconductors by 5 to 6 steps; gypsum stands in for anhydrite instead (barium stays at 71). Twelve GregTech dark concrete items fell out of reach in the same pair of dumps: that is GregTech's arbitrary concrete block (*Upstream issues*), not this change. `kubejs/server_scripts/compat/sea_ores.js` removes the nine furnace recipes that smelt a raw sea ore straight into an ingot or dust (`gtceu:smelting/smelt_raw_*_ore_to_ingot` for chalcopyrite, pyrite, sphalerite, galena, barite, pyrolusite, cobaltite, pitchblende and pollucite, run by the low pressure steam furnace at step 16 or Create's fan at 24), so the ores go through GregTech's machines, the way `metal_gates.js` keeps iron on the bloomery. **Stretches from real geochemistry:** the sulfides (copper, zinc, lead, iron, with indium, gallium, cadmium and cobalt in the sphalerite and chalcopyrite) are real black-smoker minerals; manganese nodules and crusts really carry nickel, copper, cobalt, molybdenum, niobium and tellurium, with pyrolusite standing in for todorokite; uranium is only adsorbed on crusts (a few ppm), so pitchblende is a token; caesium is enriched in arc vent fluids but pollucite is a pegmatite mineral, so that is a token too; gypsum stands in for anhydrite. Neon, krypton, xenon and naquadah stay with the planned planets. The quests: chapter "The Deep Sea" in `tools/quests/route.json` (diving gear, nodules, crust, a vent field, niobium, indium), and the checker's actions `mine_vent_chimney`, `mine_anhydrite_chimney`, `sweep_nodule_bed` and `mine_mn_crust` and `pick_loose_nodule` (`tools/recipes/progression.json`; the last needs the diving gear and no tool) say what a diver needs. Accepted in `tools/worldgen_baseline.json` as new chunks only. |
| Underwater ruins | **Hopo Better Underwater Ruins 1.2.1b and Underwater Village 1.0.2, fitted like the other structure mods** (both `side = both`, packwiz links to Modrinth's files; both are datapacks in a jar, Hopo's with no code and Underwater Village's with one network class; licences: Hopo's jar says free to include unmodified in a public modpack while Modrinth says LGPL-3.0, and Underwater Village is All Rights Reserved, so never repack either: every change is data in `kubejs/data/`). **Where they start:** their biome lists name vanilla oceans, so the pack points them at TFC's (`kubejs/data/zhopo/tags/worldgen/biome/has_structure/*` replaces Hopo's; the seventeen Underwater Village structure files are copied with `#pack:ocean_ruins/{deep,shelf,any}`: deep ocean, deep ocean atolls and the ridge for cities and deep structures, plus the ocean, reef and atolls for the shallow ones). TFC's ocean biomes have one temperature everywhere, so the cold and warm variants are split by climate (`tfc:climate`, up to 14 C cold and from 14 C warm). **Density** (as shipped Hopo's small ruins come every 11 chunks and Underwater Village's 17 sets make about one start per 10 km^2 of ocean): Hopo's city 80/70 chunks (spacing/separation; the mod's own 42/38), small ruins 24/6, fossils 30/6; Underwater Village at twice its spacing. Measured at 38.5 N 12 W (a 2,048-block window, 1,678 km^2): 99 starts, 59 per 1,000 km^2 (41 warm small ruins, 25 fossils, 1 Hopo city, 32 Underwater Village in 11 kinds). **The skin** (`pack_world` 1.6.0) covers `hopo` and `underwater_village`, with the new `sea_water` key (`kubejs/data/pack/pack_world/structure_skin/sea_water.json`): in those namespaces, where the structure's chunk lies in a `#tfc:is_ocean` biome (a ruin in a lake or river keeps river water), vanilla water becomes TFC salt water (same level); blocks the table removes (kelp, seagrass, bubble columns, sea pickles) leave salt water where they held water; a waterlogged block mapped to a TFC block keeps its water as salt water when the block can hold it (`IFluidLoggable`) and is left as the structure wrote it when it cannot (it would otherwise leave an air pocket); and air the templates wrote that is open to the sea is turned back into sea. Sealed rooms stay dry (`sea_water_fill_air`): 37 of the 228 templates hold sealed air (5,780 cells in all; Hopo's city has vaults of 966 and 911 cells and a hall of 447, Underwater Village's temple dungeon 41 rooms and 1,453 cells), and a room that crosses a chunk border is flooded in the chunk where it reaches the border. ## Known gaps (from the recipe explorer)

The first blockers (no vanilla andesite for Create, no vanilla furnace for GregTech's steam age, duplicate materials across mods, no TFC nuggets, no dried kelp) are fixed by steps 1–5. Still open:

- Create's blaze burner needs the Nether (netherrack, a blaze). Its job, heating basins, is covered by Woodencog's forge-heated basins (see "Create addons"), so the blaze burner itself is only missing for blaze cakes.
- GregTech-only elements still without a source: neon, krypton and xenon (Nether/End air), and naquadah. Niobium, indium, caesium and uranium (nuclear fuel) now come from the sea floor (*Deep sea*: steps 38, 65, 19 and 60). None is on the path to the IV hull; they matter for some side machines, nuclear power and LuV+.
- Aeronautics' levitite needs End stone (no End access planned yet).
- Some starts are far from igneous extrusive rock, so andesite alloy also takes diorite, and the Andesite Alloy quest tells players where to look (loose rocks, volcanic regions, dikes). TFC picks rock per patch of about 1 km, from a list set by the patch's zone: volcanic zones are mostly extrusive at the surface, ordinary land 4 patches in 25, mountain zones never. Real World draws the volcanic and mountain zones from its 28 km tectonics map, so at 20 m per block those zones are 8.6 times wider in blocks, while the patches are not. Measured with `tools/rocksample.py` (it asks TFC's rock generator directly; checked against five generated 2 km worlds, 98% of columns match, the rest are dikes): for 300 random land starts the nearest extrusive surface rock on land is a median 621 blocks away, 72% within 1,000, 92% within 2,000, at most 5,344. Mountain starts are the tail (48% within 2,000), and digging 100 blocks down adds about 1%. Worst named starts: Lhasa 4,776, Toronto 3,080, Manaus 2,510, Kathmandu 2,338; Rome, Delhi, Auckland, Mexico City and Buenos Aires start on it. Diorite, andesite's intrusive twin, is close almost everywhere: TFC's diorite dikes (pipes about 30 blocks across, about one per 300 chunks, most topping out 1–5 blocks under the ground; loose rocks don't show them) put it a median 110–150 blocks from a start, and within 512 blocks of all 500 starts sampled in the five test worlds; mountain zones also have diorite as a surface rock.
- AE2, Sophisticated Storage and the newer Create addons keep what needs the Nether, the End or a heart of the sea unreachable: shulker boxes, the alchemy and feeding upgrades, the ender linker, the blaze enchanter and forger, Aquatic Ambitions' conduit, trident and prismarine chain, the thruster upgrade chain (blaze burner), and the creative and debug items. Gadgets & Gizmos 2.0.0-Beta-2 logs one bad Sable physics file and skips optional compat mixins (`tools/known_issues.json`).
- Group two's unreachable on purpose: backpack feeding, alchemy, infinity and inception upgrades, bee frames and upgrades that need the Nether, End or a heart of the sea, MEGA's cells for mods that are not in the pack, ExtendedAE's infinity cells, the block picker card, pocket computers' golden apple (replaced by a battery), the command computer and the vanilla diamond turtle upgrades (no vanilla diamond tools).
- Exploration rewards (group three): Paragliders' generated and horned statues do not exist in this pack (the goddess statue is crafted); Little Joys' gold and drop rushes and Comforts' shaped nail recipe do nothing; six artifacts are unreachable on purpose (digging claws, pickaxe heater, rooted boots, everlasting beef, helium flamingo, universal attractor) and the full campsite is not placed (its barrel, furnaces and anvil are vanilla blocks hard-coded in the mod); the construction stick's destruction, replacement and unbreakable upgrades cannot be made, and the enduring mixture needs a golden carrot. Nothing in this group was tested in a client: titles, the artifact slots in Curios, the upgrades of the sticks, sleeping bags and hammocks with TFC's sleep rules, and whether TFC's collapse and support rules matter to the sticks' placing.
- Cosmetic: Create Deco's mossy bricks need moss blocks, which TFC worlds lack (the lush caves use TFC's mossy cobble; the glow berries its green lamps need now grow on cave vines in warm, wet caves); netherrack and netherite decoration needs the Nether; waxing copper needs honeycomb.

## Upstream issues to track

- **Almost Unified 1.4.2 doesn't unify GregTech 8.0 recipes.** Its GregTech unifier reads the old capability keys (`item`), while GregTech 8.0 writes `gtceu:item`. Workaround: `kubejs/server_scripts/unification/gregtech.js` asks Almost Unified for each tag's winning item and applies it to GregTech recipes.
- **GregTech 8.0 drops its recycling recipes when any KubeJS recipe script exists.** `GTRecipes` skips recycling generation when KubeJS has recipe listeners, but the 1.21 KubeJS plugin no longer runs it afterwards (1.20.1 did, in `injectRuntimeRecipes`). About 3,300 macerator/arc furnace recycling recipes are missing; none are needed for progression. Recheck with a dump comparison after GregTech updates.
- **JEI is pinned to 19.44.0.413.** GregTech 8.0 bundles ModularUI 3.3.1, whose mixin into JEI's `RecipeSlot` needs a field (`allIngredients`) that JEI 19.50 removed. With a newer JEI the mixin fails when the class first loads, and EMI shows "render failure" for every recipe it gets from JEI plugins (all of Create's). `tools/smoke_checks.js` loads those JEI classes on the test server, so the smoke test fails if a JEI or GregTech update breaks this again. Unpin once ModularUI supports JEI 19.50+.
- **GregTech 8.0 nightlies ship no names for recipe categories and types** (`recipe_category.gtceu.*`, `gtceu.<type>`), so recipe viewers show raw keys. Workaround: `kubejs/assets/gtceu/lang/en_us.json`; drop entries once GregTech ships them.
- **TFC's 5–8 hardened clay → clay ball recipes give 20–32 balls**, more than the 16 per stack TFC itself allows, so they failed whenever recipes reloaded in a session (`/reload`, opening a second world). Disabled by data overrides in `kubejs/data/tfc/recipe/crafting/clay_ball_[5-8].json`; 1–4 clay at a time still works. Drop the overrides if TFC fixes them.

- **GregTech 8.0 picks an arbitrary block for its concrete recipes.** `gtceu:fluid_solidifier/solidify_concrete_block` and the recycling recipes named after its output take any member of `#c:storage_blocks/concrete`, and which one differs from dump to dump (a vanilla colour or `gtceu:light_concrete`), so concrete dust is step 26 or 38 in the checker. Nothing is gated by it, and Roads and Roofs' concrete is a separate chain that is in no GregTech tag.
- **TFC Structure Compat's loot tables use TFC 3 item ids** (`tfc:food/bread`, `dried_beef`, `tfc:ore/native_copper`...): four of them (`minecraft:chests/abandoned_mineshaft`, `mvs:general`, `mvs:houses_common`, `mvs:mineshaft/common`) failed to parse and would have given empty chests. All of the structure mods' loot is now the pack's own (`tools/loot/`), so nothing reads them.
- **TFC Huts ships recipes for a mod that isn't in the pack** (Minecraft Comes Alive), logged as parse errors and skipped (`tools/known_issues.json`; its Comforts recipes load now that Comforts is in the pack, apart from a shaped nail recipe with rows of unequal width), and overrides a few vanilla and TFC recipes (anvil, stonecutter, grindstone, fletching table, glass bottle, TFC gem and vanilla gem conversions, golden apple); the progression checker accepts them (*Implementation order*, step 8).
- **Moog's Voyager Structures must stay at 5.0.11** (pinned in `mods/moogs-voyager-structures.pw.toml`): TFC Structure Compat overrides structure definitions that name six template pools 5.0.14 removed, and the server then refuses to load ("Unbound values in registry template_pool"). Lift the pin once the compat catches up, with `worldgen_check.py`.
- **Addon recipes written for older versions.** Woodencog's red steel diving gear welding and quartz milling use pre-1.21 result formats, its mud brick saw recipes use TFC 1.20's soil names (TFC 4 renamed loam/silt to soil orders such as alfisol), and Create Deco's placard washing names its dye as an item stack. Fixed or switched off by data overrides in `kubejs/data/woodencog/` and `kubejs/data/createdeco/` (mud bricks redone for the current soils in `compat/create_addons.js`). Factory Logistics ships recipes for a barrel packager it doesn't register and a Mekanism gauge; both are switched off in `kubejs/data/create_factory_logistics/`. Drop the overrides when the mods catch up.
